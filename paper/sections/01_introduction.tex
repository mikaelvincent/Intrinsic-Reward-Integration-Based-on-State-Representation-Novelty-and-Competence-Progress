\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Deep reinforcement learning agents often learn effectively when dense extrinsic rewards provide informative gradients throughout training, but performance can degrade sharply when rewards are sparse, delayed, or shaped in ways that do not guide early exploration.
Intrinsic-reward methods address this by augmenting the environment reward with an auxiliary signal that encourages behaviors such as novelty-seeking, uncertainty reduction, or state-space coverage.
Classic approaches include count-based exploration \cite{strehl-2006} and Bayesian uncertainty estimates \cite{osband-2016}, while modern deep RL methods commonly employ learned predictive errors as intrinsic signals \cite{pathak-2017,burda-2019-icm,burda-2019-rnd}.
Despite strong empirical results, a recurring failure mode is \emph{unproductive curiosity}: intrinsic objectives can remain high in regions that are difficult to model, even when those regions yield little progress toward competent task behavior.

A large family of exploration methods is motivated by \emph{learning progress} \cite{schmidhuber-2007}, which rewards improvements in a model's predictive ability rather than raw prediction error.
Learning progress can distinguish regions where the agent is actively learning from regions that are either already predictable or intrinsically noisy.
However, practical instantiations must address (i) how to localize progress in high-dimensional observations, (ii) how to combine progress with other exploration signals that encourage diverse transitions, and (iii) how to avoid overstimulating regions where high error persists but learning stagnates.

We propose \emph{Gated Learning-Progress Exploration} (GLPE), a lightweight intrinsic-reward family that integrates learning progress and feature-space impact under a unified, on-policy training procedure.
GLPE maintains a simple online partition of a learned latent feature space and tracks forward-model prediction error within each region using short- and long-horizon exponential moving averages.
Region-local learning progress is then defined as the positive discrepancy between these averages, indicating whether prediction error is decreasing in that region.
We combine this progress term with a feature-space impact signal based on the magnitude of latent-state change, yielding an intrinsic objective that favors transitions that both change the representation and occur in regions where the model is improving.

To mitigate unproductive curiosity, GLPE introduces an optional region-specific \emph{gate} that suppresses intrinsic rewards in regions whose prediction error remains high while learning progress is low.
This gate is intentionally simple: it compares region-local progress to a robust global median threshold and disables shaping only after repeated unproductive visits.
The resulting mechanism is not intended to optimize an information-theoretic objective; rather, it is intended as a targeted guardrail against unproductive curiosity.
We also study \emph{GLPE (no gate)}, which uses the same base intrinsic score without any gating and serves as a low-friction default.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We introduce the GLPE family, combining region-local learning progress with feature-space impact under lightweight online normalization.
    \item We propose a simple gating mechanism that suppresses intrinsic shaping in high-error, low-progress regions, and study its effect relative to an ungated variant.
    \item We provide an empirical study on a five-environment Gymnasium suite with a common PPO backbone, reporting both step-normalized and wall-clock-normalized comparisons, along with ablations and diagnostics of intrinsic components and computational overhead.
\end{itemize}

We evaluate on \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3}, and three MuJoCo locomotion tasks (\textsc{Ant-v5}, \textsc{HalfCheetah-v5}, \textsc{Humanoid-v5}), comparing against intrinsic baselines including ICM \cite{pathak-2017}, RND \cite{burda-2019-rnd}, RIDE \cite{raileanu-2020}, and RIAC \cite{baranes-2009}.
Across these benchmarks, GLPE (no gate) matches or closely tracks the strongest baseline across both step-normalized and wall-clock-normalized views, while the full gated variant is most helpful on the sparse-reward \textsc{MountainCar-v0} task, where it improves reliability and reduces instability relative to ungated shaping.
