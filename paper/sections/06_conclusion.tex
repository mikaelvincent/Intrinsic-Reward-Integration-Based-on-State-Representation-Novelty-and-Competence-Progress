\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work studied intrinsic-reward shaping for exploration in control tasks where extrinsic feedback can be sparse, delayed, or insufficient to guide early learning.
Building on learning-progress exploration and feature-space impact, we introduced the \emph{GLPE family}: \textbf{GLPE (no gate)} combines region-local learning progress with feature-space impact under lightweight online normalization, while \textbf{GLPE} augments the same base signal with a region-specific gate that can set intrinsic reward to zero in high-error, low-progress regions.

Across the five-environment Gymnasium suite and a shared PPO backbone (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}), \textbf{GLPE (no gate)} is the most consistent default, remaining competitive with the strongest baseline under both step-normalized and wall-clock-normalized views (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
Practically, it provides a low-friction way to bias exploration toward transitions that both \emph{change} the learned representation and occur in regions where the dynamics model is actively improving.
The full gated variant is best interpreted as an optional guardrail: it is most helpful in sparse-reward regimes (most clearly on \textsc{MountainCar-v0}), where it provides a targeted guardrail by selectively disabling unproductive shaping, and it can be overly conservative in high-variance settings.
The cosine taper schedule further makes exploration support intentionally transient, smoothly reducing reliance on intrinsic rewards late in training while preserving the early-learning benefits of intrinsic shaping.

Our study is limited to vector-observation benchmarks and on-policy PPO, and (as with most exploration bonuses) the intrinsic shaping we use is not policy-invariant.
The gate also introduces additional computation and thresholding that can be unnecessary---or counterproductive in high-variance settings---relative to \textbf{GLPE (no gate)}.
Future work can therefore focus on extending the GLPE family to richer observation modalities and alternative training regimes, and on making the gated variant cheaper and more adaptive (e.g., via cached or more efficient robust statistics) without changing the underlying intrinsic objective.
