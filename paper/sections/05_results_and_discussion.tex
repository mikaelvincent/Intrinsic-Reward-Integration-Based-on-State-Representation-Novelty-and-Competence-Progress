\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate the GLPE family and intrinsic-reward baselines on the five-environment suite from Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}.
All plots and aggregates use the deterministic evaluation protocol: at each checkpoint we run 20 episodes per seed and report the mean \emph{extrinsic} return (i.e., the task reward without intrinsic shaping).
Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines} summarizes learning dynamics, Figure~\ref{fig:steps_to_threshold_baselines} reports sample efficiency at fixed reward thresholds, Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_intrinsic_taper} visualizes the intrinsic scheduling used by the GLPE family, and Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines} summarizes final-checkpoint performance.

Across the suite, GLPE and GLPE (no gate) remain competitive with established exploration baselines while offering a favorable robustness--complexity tradeoff.
On \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, GLPE achieves the best final return (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}) and reaches the solved threshold of $-110$ reliably and early (10/10 seeds; median \approx{}\(3.46\times10^5\) steps).
This gap to the strongest baseline is practically meaningful: the best baseline median solve time is \approx{}\(4.41\times10^5\) steps (9/10 seeds), and several intrinsic baselines show larger variance in when (or whether) they cross the threshold.

On the higher-dimensional locomotion tasks, GLPE (no gate) is a strong and low-friction default.
On \textsc{HalfCheetah-v5}, both GLPE variants surpass the $6000$ solve threshold in all seeds, finishing around $6.9\,$k return (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}), and their learning curves remain close to the strongest intrinsic baselines throughout training.
While RIDE attains the highest mean final return on this task, GLPE remains close in both final performance and learning speed, with the additional benefit that the same intrinsic construction transfers without task-specific mechanism changes.
On \textsc{Humanoid-v5}, where variance is substantial across all methods, GLPE (no gate) is statistically indistinguishable from the strongest baseline (ICM) in mean final return (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}).
The gated variant improves markedly over vanilla PPO but is more conservative here, suggesting that turning off gating can be a pragmatic default when the state space is large and the risk of prematurely suppressing useful exploration is higher.

\textsc{Ant-v5} highlights a complementary regime where many methods approach the solved threshold.
GLPE and GLPE (no gate) finish slightly below $5000$ on average but still cross the threshold in 7/8 seeds, indicating that the method remains competitive even when dense extrinsic reward already provides strong guidance.
Finally, on \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3} (solved threshold $300$), several baselines reliably solve, and GLPE (no gate) approaches the threshold with a comparatively tight confidence interval.
However, fewer GLPE runs cross the strict solved threshold than the best baseline, suggesting that on already well-shaped tasks the primary advantage of GLPE can be earlier progress rather than consistently higher final performance.

The threshold-based analysis in Figure~\ref{fig:steps_to_threshold_baselines} complements the learning curves by separating \emph{how fast} a method reaches a target score from \emph{how often} it reaches it.
GLPE's main strength is consistent and efficient progress on exploration-limited tasks: on MountainCar, it reaches all three threshold levels quickly with near-perfect reliability.
On HalfCheetah, GLPE reaches the solved threshold in all seeds, indicating that the locality-aware learning-progress signal does not sacrifice robustness even in continuous-control settings.
On BipedalWalker and Humanoid, the strict thresholds expose variance across methods: several baselines solve BipedalWalker reliably, while on Humanoid all methods solve in only a subset of seeds.

Because the GLPE family anneals intrinsic shaping with a cosine taper (Section~\ref{sec:method}), its intrinsic contribution is intentionally transient.
Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_intrinsic_taper} shows that the underlying intrinsic signal remains nonzero, but the applied intrinsic reward smoothly decays to (near) zero by the configured end of the taper.
This scheduling complements gating by keeping early exploration incentives while reducing the risk that persistent intrinsic bonuses distort late-training fine-tuning.

Beyond returns, practical usability depends on computational overhead.
Figure~\ref{fig:timing_breakdown} shows that the added intrinsic components typically remain a minority of per-update wall-clock time, with environment stepping and PPO optimization dominating on the more expensive tasks.
For the gated variant, one identifiable cost is recomputing robust global medians used to set gating thresholds.
Table~\ref{tab:gate_cache_microbenchmark} shows that a simple refresh-interval cache yields a $4.83\times$ speedup in this specific subroutine, suggesting that the gate can be made inexpensive without changing the overall algorithmic structure.
Taken together, these results indicate that GLPE (no gate) provides a competitive default intrinsic signal, while gating offers targeted robustness gains on exploration-limited tasks such as MountainCar.
