\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results_and_discussion}

We evaluate GLPE on the five-task suite in Table~\ref{tab:env_suite}, comparing against PPO without intrinsic reward (Vanilla) and four intrinsic-reward baselines (ICM, RND, RIDE, RIAC).
We measure performance using the mean episodic return from offline evaluation runs, which use 20 episodes per checkpoint with the deterministic policy mode (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup:evaluation}).
Unless stated otherwise, we report mean returns across seeds together with 95\% bootstrap confidence intervals (2{,}000 resamples), computed on the per-seed performance at the final checkpoint.

\input{figures/eval_learning_curves}

Figure~\ref{fig:eval_learning_curves} summarizes learning dynamics on the full suite.
On dense-reward locomotion tasks (\textsc{BipedalWalker}, \textsc{HalfCheetah}, \textsc{Ant}), Vanilla PPO already reaches strong scores, so intrinsic methods primarily affect learning speed and stability rather than enabling qualitatively new behaviors.
In this regime, GLPE remains competitive with established baselines but does not uniformly dominate.
For example, on \textsc{HalfCheetah} GLPE and GLPE (no gate) achieve returns close to RIDE by the end of training, while on \textsc{Ant} RIAC attains the highest mean return.

The most consistent gains from GLPE appear on \textsc{MountainCar}, where rewards are sparse and progress depends on discovering a momentum-building strategy.
Here, GLPE reaches and exceeds the solved threshold reliably, and its final return is both higher and less variable than the strongest baseline in our comparison.
Conversely, on \textsc{BipedalWalker} the gated variant underperforms Vanilla and the best intrinsic baselines, while GLPE (no gate) closes much of the gap.
This pattern suggests a tradeoff in the region-level gate: suppressing intrinsic reward can reduce interference once exploitation is appropriate, but it can also remove useful exploration pressure in tasks where local optima persist late into training.

\textsc{Humanoid} exhibits substantial across-seed variability for all intrinsic methods, reflected in wide confidence intervals.
In this setting, GLPE (no gate) matches the mean performance of ICM, whereas the gated variant trends lower.
Given the small number of seeds (Table~\ref{tab:env_suite}), these differences should be interpreted as directional rather than definitive.

Overall, GLPE offers a competitive intrinsic signal that improves performance on sparse-reward \textsc{MountainCar} and remains near the strongest baselines on \textsc{HalfCheetah}, but the current gating scheme is not uniformly beneficial across the locomotion suite.
We return to these tradeoffs when discussing limitations and opportunities for improving gate calibration and robustness.
