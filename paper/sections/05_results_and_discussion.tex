\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We compare GLPE and GLPE (no gate) against Vanilla PPO and four intrinsic-reward baselines (ICM, RND, RIDE, RIAC) on the five-task suite from Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}.
Unless otherwise stated, we report \emph{extrinsic} evaluation return under the deterministic offline evaluation protocol (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup:evaluation}).

\subsection{Learning curves and final performance}

Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines} shows evaluation learning curves versus environment steps.
Across tasks, GLPE (no gate) tracks the best-performing baseline closely and exhibits stable between-seed behavior.
The gated variant differs mainly in sparse-reward or exploration-sensitive regimes: it is most beneficial on \textsc{MountainCar-v0} and can be overly conservative on \textsc{Humanoid-v5}, where variance across seeds dominates.

Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines} summarizes final-checkpoint return, while Table~\ref{tab:auc_steps_glpe_vs_baselines} reports step-AUC, which rewards both early learning and sustained performance over the fixed interaction budget.

\input{tables/final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}
\input{tables/auc_steps_glpe_vs_baselines}

On \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, GLPE attains the best final return and the strongest step-AUC among all compared methods, and both GLPE variants solve in all seeds (Tables~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines} and~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
On \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3}, GLPE (no gate) improves rapidly and remains competitive by step-AUC, but many runs plateau just below the solved threshold of $300$, making solved counts sensitive to small differences near the cutoff (Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
On the MuJoCo locomotion tasks, where extrinsic reward is comparatively dense, the two GLPE variants behave similarly and remain within a modest gap of the strongest intrinsic baseline under both summaries (Tables~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines} and~\ref{tab:auc_steps_glpe_vs_baselines}).
\textsc{Humanoid-v5} exhibits particularly high variance, with wide confidence intervals across all methods, so method rankings are not stable under bootstrap uncertainty.

Some diagnostic plots additionally include auxiliary GLPE variants (e.g., cached medians and single-component ablations) to isolate computational and signal contributions; we treat these as supporting analyses rather than primary baselines.

\subsection{Ablations: impact vs.\ learning progress}

To probe which components drive performance in different regimes, Table~\ref{tab:glpe_component_ablation_returns} reports final returns for GLPE and two single-term ablations on representative tasks.

\input{tables/glpe_component_ablation_returns}

On sparse-reward \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, combining impact and learning progress is critical: each single-term variant falls substantially short of full GLPE.
In contrast, on dense-reward \textsc{Ant-v5} the impact-only variant is competitive, and on near-threshold \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3} the LP-only variant comes closest to the solved cutoff.
These patterns motivate the combined score as a robust default when the ``right'' intrinsic driver is task-dependent.

\subsection{Thresholded reliability and steps-to-threshold}

Learning curves can obscure whether a method is merely slower or is failing entirely.
We therefore evaluate reliability at fixed return thresholds using steps-to-threshold statistics (Figure~\ref{fig:steps_to_threshold_baselines}) and success counts (Tables~\ref{tab:half_solved_threshold_stats} and~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
We use task-specific solved thresholds (\textsc{MountainCar-v0}: $-110$, \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3}: $300$, \textsc{Ant-v5}: $5{,}000$, \textsc{HalfCheetah-v5}: $6{,}000$, \textsc{Humanoid-v5}: $3{,}000$) and additionally report results at 25\% and 50\% of these levels.

\input{figures/steps_to_threshold_baselines}
\input{tables/half_solved_threshold_stats}
\input{tables/solved_threshold_stats}

At the 50\% threshold, GLPE (no gate) reaches the target in all seeds on four tasks and in 4/5 seeds on \textsc{Humanoid-v5}, matching the best baseline reach count on every environment (Table~\ref{tab:half_solved_threshold_stats}).
The gap between 50\% and 100\% success is concentrated in high-variance or near-cutoff regimes: \textsc{Humanoid-v5} and \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3} show many runs that reach competent behavior without consistently clearing the full solved cutoff (Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
On \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, by contrast, both GLPE variants reach the solved threshold quickly and reliably, and several baselines exhibit occasional failures that remain near the minimum return (Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines}).

\subsection{Intrinsic shaping dynamics and gating behavior}

The GLPE family is designed as a transient exploration aid rather than a permanent auxiliary objective.
Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_intrinsic_taper} visualizes the cosine taper schedule used to anneal the applied intrinsic reward late in training, and Figure~\ref{fig:train_reward_decomp_baselines} decomposes the training-time rollout reward into extrinsic and intrinsic components.
Compared to baselines that maintain a nonzero intrinsic term throughout training, GLPE and GLPE (no gate) smoothly reduce the intrinsic contribution while the underlying (untapered) intrinsic signal remains present.

\input{figures/glpe_intrinsic_taper}
\input{figures/training_reward_decomposition_baselines}

To make the gate's effect concrete, Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_gate_map} shows state-space projections of final-checkpoint trajectories with timesteps colored by whether intrinsic shaping is active.
Across environments, gate-off points occupy a small and typically localized subset of the visited distribution.
This supports the interpretation of gating as a targeted guardrail: it suppresses shaping only in narrow regions that appear persistently high-error and low-progress, rather than globally weakening exploration.

\input{figures/glpe_gate_map}

\subsection{Wall-clock efficiency and computational overhead}

Intrinsic rewards can change not only sample efficiency but also end-to-end training cost.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval_auc_time} reports wall-clock AUC of evaluation return under a common per-environment time horizon (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup:efficiency}), ensuring that slower methods are not advantaged by extrapolation beyond measured runtime.
Across \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3}, \textsc{HalfCheetah-v5}, \textsc{Ant-v5}, and \textsc{Humanoid-v5}, GLPE (no gate) remains close to the strongest baseline under this wall-clock view.
Full GLPE is typically further back, consistent with the additional computation required by gating and robust statistics.

\input{figures/eval_auc_time}

Figure~\ref{fig:timing_breakdown} decomposes per-update runtime into environment interaction, PPO optimization, and intrinsic components.
On the more expensive MuJoCo tasks, environment stepping and PPO optimization dominate total time, making intrinsic overhead a secondary factor; on \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, where environment interaction is cheap, intrinsic overhead plays a larger role in wall-clock comparisons.

\input{figures/timing_breakdown}

Because cached medians are slightly stale and can change gating decisions, we treat caching as an optional engineering knob rather than part of the core GLPE comparison.
