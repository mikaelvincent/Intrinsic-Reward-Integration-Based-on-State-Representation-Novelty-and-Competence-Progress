\section{Results and discussion}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate the GLPE family and intrinsic-reward baselines on the five-environment suite from Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup}.
All plots and aggregates use the deterministic evaluation protocol: at each checkpoint we run 20 episodes per seed and report the mean \emph{extrinsic} return (i.e., the task reward without intrinsic shaping).
Unless otherwise noted, each environment uses 8 random seeds (10 for \textsc{MountainCar-v0} and 5 for \textsc{Humanoid-v5}).
We primarily interpret learning curves (Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines}), strict solved-threshold reliability and steps-to-threshold (Figure~\ref{fig:steps_to_threshold_baselines} and Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}), and wall-clock efficiency via time-AUC under a common budget (Figure~\ref{fig:eval_auc_time}).
GLPE-specific diagnostics visualize the intrinsic taper schedule (Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_intrinsic_taper}) and where gating suppresses shaping along final-checkpoint trajectories (Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_gate_map}).
Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines} reports final-checkpoint returns, and Table~\ref{tab:glpe_component_ablation_returns} summarizes intrinsic-component ablations on three representative tasks.

Across the suite, GLPE and GLPE (no gate) remain competitive with established exploration baselines.
The gated variant provides a conservative ``safety valve'' against unproductive intrinsic shaping, while GLPE (no gate) is a simpler default that avoids gating-specific overhead and failure modes.
On \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, GLPE achieves the best final return among all evaluated methods (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}).
Both GLPE variants solve the task in all seeds, reaching the $-110$ solved threshold in \(\approx 0.35\,\mathrm{M}\) median steps (Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
In contrast, several baselines exhibit occasional catastrophic failures that remain near the minimum return (Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines}), which lowers both final-return averages and solved-threshold reliability.
Among baselines, RIDE reaches the same threshold fastest (\(\approx 0.44\,\mathrm{M}\) median steps) but still fails in 1/10 seeds (Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).

On the MuJoCo locomotion tasks, GLPE (no gate) is a strong and low-friction default.
On \textsc{HalfCheetah-v5}, both GLPE variants reach the $6000$ solved threshold in all seeds and finish around $6.9\,$k mean return (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}), with learning curves that track the strongest intrinsic baselines throughout training.
While RIDE attains the highest mean final return on this task, GLPE remains close in both final performance and learning speed using the same two-term intrinsic formulation.
On \textsc{Humanoid-v5}, variance is substantial across all methods: a small number of seeds achieve high returns while others remain far below the solved threshold, leading to wide confidence intervals (Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines}).
In this regime, GLPE (no gate) matches the strongest baseline (ICM) in mean final return within overlapping confidence intervals (Table~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}); correspondingly, both methods reach the $3000$ solved threshold in only 2/5 seeds (Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats}).
The gated variant improves over vanilla PPO on average but lags GLPE (no gate) here, suggesting that disabling gating can be a pragmatic choice when suppressing exploration too early is costly.

\textsc{Ant-v5} highlights a complementary regime where many methods approach the solved threshold.
GLPE and GLPE (no gate) finish slightly below $5000$ on average but still reach the threshold at least once in 7/8 seeds, indicating that the method remains competitive even when dense extrinsic reward already provides strong guidance.
Finally, on \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3} (solved threshold $300$), several baselines reliably solve, and GLPE (no gate) approaches the threshold with a comparatively tight confidence interval.
However, GLPE (no gate) crosses the strict solved threshold in only 2/8 seeds (versus 7/8 for vanilla PPO and 8/8 for RIAC), and the gated variant reaches the threshold in 4/8 seeds but with lower mean final return (Tables~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats} and~\ref{tab:final_returns_glpe_vs_baselines}).
For GLPE (no gate), the final-return confidence interval straddles the $300$ threshold, so small between-seed differences translate into large changes in ``solved'' counts.
For the gated variant, Figure~\ref{fig:eval_curves_baselines} shows an occasional collapse to low returns in a minority of seeds, which lowers the mean despite some successful runs.
Overall, this pattern suggests a near-threshold sensitivity on well-shaped tasks: the GLPE family can reach stable, near-solved performance, but does not consistently provide an additional push past the final threshold without further task-specific tuning.

Diagnostic ablations within the GLPE family---run on \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3}, and \textsc{Ant-v5}---help explain these regimes.
Table~\ref{tab:glpe_component_ablation_returns} isolates the two intrinsic components by setting impact-only ($\alpha_{\mathrm{LP}}{=}0$) or LP-only ($\alpha_{\mathrm{impact}}{=}0$).
On \textsc{MountainCar-v0}, both single-term variants fall well short of GLPE, consistent with impact and learning progress acting as complementary drivers of exploration in a sparse-reward setting.
In contrast, on \textsc{Ant-v5} the impact-only variant slightly exceeds GLPE in mean final return, and on \textsc{BipedalWalker-v3} the LP-only variant comes closest to the solved threshold.
Together, these ablations suggest that while a single component can be sufficient in some dense-reward regimes, the combined GLPE score provides a robust default that avoids committing to an environment-specific choice of intrinsic signal.

The threshold-based analysis in Figure~\ref{fig:steps_to_threshold_baselines} complements the learning curves by separating \emph{how fast} a method reaches a target score from \emph{how often} it reaches it.
Table~\ref{tab:solved_threshold_stats} summarizes the strict solved-threshold (100\%) outcomes: GLPE's most pronounced advantage is on the exploration-limited MountainCar task, while on the locomotion tasks GLPE (no gate) remains broadly competitive with strong baselines but inherits the cross-seed variance that dominates Humanoid and the near-threshold sensitivity of BipedalWalker.

Because the GLPE family anneals intrinsic shaping with a cosine taper (Section~\ref{sec:method}), its intrinsic contribution is intentionally transient.
Figure~\ref{fig:glpe_intrinsic_taper} shows that the underlying intrinsic signal remains nonzero, but the applied intrinsic reward smoothly decays to (near) zero by the configured end of the taper.
This scheduling complements gating by keeping early exploration incentives while reducing the risk that persistent intrinsic bonuses distort late-training fine-tuning.

Computational efficiency can alter conclusions when methods are compared under a fixed wall-clock budget rather than a fixed interaction budget.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval_auc_time} summarizes this perspective using an area-under-curve (AUC) metric of evaluation return versus cumulative training time (Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup:efficiency}), with each environment using a common time horizon to avoid extrapolating slower methods.
The GLPE family remains competitive in this view: GLPE retains its MountainCar advantage, and GLPE (no gate) stays close to the strongest baselines on the locomotion tasks.

To interpret these wall-clock tradeoffs, Figure~\ref{fig:timing_breakdown} decomposes per-update runtime into environment interaction, PPO optimization, and intrinsic components.
The intrinsic segments typically remain a minority of wall-clock time, with environment stepping and PPO optimization dominating on the more expensive tasks.
For the gated variant, one identifiable cost is recomputing robust global medians used to set gating thresholds.
Table~\ref{tab:gate_cache_microbenchmark} shows that a simple refresh-interval cache yields a $4.83\times$ speedup in this specific subroutine, suggesting that the gate can be made inexpensive without changing the overall algorithmic structure.
Because caching uses slightly stale medians and can therefore change gating decisions, we treat it as an optional engineering knob rather than part of the core GLPE comparison.
Taken together, these results indicate that GLPE (no gate) provides a competitive default intrinsic signal, while gating offers targeted robustness gains on exploration-limited tasks such as MountainCar.
