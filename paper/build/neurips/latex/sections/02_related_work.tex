\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Intrinsic motivation in reinforcement learning is commonly framed as augmenting sparse extrinsic feedback with an internally generated signal that encourages exploration \cite{singh-2004,barto-2012,oudeyer-2007A,oudeyer-2007B}.
Proposed intrinsic objectives span novelty and visitation bonuses \cite{bellemare-2016,tang-2016,ostrovski-2017}, information-gain and uncertainty reduction \cite{mohamed-2015,houthooft-2016}, and learned prediction-error curiosity \cite{stadie-2015,pathak-2017,burda-2018A,burda-2018B}.
Many modern methods apply these bonuses as reward shaping during policy optimization; unlike potential-based shaping, intrinsic rewards can change the optimal policy and are therefore evaluated empirically \cite{ng-1999}.

Prediction-error bonuses have been particularly effective in high-dimensional settings.
ICM \cite{pathak-2017} learns a forward model in a learned feature space, while RND \cite{burda-2018B} trains a predictor against a fixed random target.
Related approaches measure novelty via episodic memory \cite{savinov-2018}, model disagreement \cite{pathak-2019}, or combinations of episodic and lifelong bonuses \cite{badia-2020}.
A known limitation is that prediction error may remain high in regions that are inherently stochastic or difficult to model, leading to exploration that is intense but not task-useful; the ``noisy TV'' phenomenon is one concrete example \cite{mavor-parker-2021}.

Learning-progress approaches instead reward improvements in a predictive model over time, encouraging experience that is learnable but not yet mastered \cite{schmidhuber-1991,oudeyer-2007A}.
In continuous control, RIAC \cite{baranes-2009} localizes progress by maintaining an adaptive partition of state space and using progress estimates to guide exploration.
The GLPE family builds on this lineage by (i) computing region-local learning progress in a learned latent space rather than in hand-chosen state coordinates, (ii) combining progress with a feature-space impact term related to controllable change \cite{raileanu-2020}, and (iii) introducing an explicit gate that suppresses intrinsic shaping when prediction error stays high but progress stalls.
This gate serves as a lightweight guardrail against unproductive curiosity rather than an alternative intrinsic objective.
