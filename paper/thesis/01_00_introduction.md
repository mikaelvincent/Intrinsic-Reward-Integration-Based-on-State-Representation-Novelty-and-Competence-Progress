## 1. Introduction

Deep reinforcement learning performs effectively when extrinsic rewards are dense and informative, but performance degrades when rewards are sparse, delayed, or weakly aligned with early exploration behavior {{CIT:3,20}}. Intrinsic motivation methods addressed this issue by augmenting task rewards with auxiliary signals that encouraged exploration when external feedback was limited {{CIT:11,12,20}}.

Existing intrinsic objectives include count and density based novelty, information gain, prediction error, and impact driven signals {{CIT:2,4,6,7,10,13,15,22}}. These approaches improved exploration in many settings, but they also showed a recurring failure mode in which high novelty or prediction error persisted in regions that did not support policy improvement. This behavior is commonly described as unproductive curiosity and is particularly problematic in sparse reward tasks {{CIT:8}}.

Learning progress based exploration offered a complementary criterion. Under this view, experience is useful when predictive performance improves over time, not only when transitions are surprising {{CIT:12,17}}. This perspective motivated mechanisms that identify regions where model learning is active and distinguish them from regions where error remains high but progress is minimal.

The present work implemented and evaluated Gated Learning Progress Exploration (GLPE), including a gated variant and a non gated variant, with a shared Proximal Policy Optimization backbone. The intrinsic score combined feature space impact and region local learning progress estimated from short and long horizon prediction error trends in an online latent space partition. The gated variant suppressed intrinsic shaping in persistently high error and low progress regions, while the non gated variant preserved the same base score without suppression.

Evaluation was conducted on MountainCar-v0, BipedalWalker-v3, Ant-v5, HalfCheetah-v5, and Humanoid-v5. Comparisons were performed against Vanilla PPO, ICM, RND, RIDE, and RIAC using a common training and evaluation protocol. Performance was analyzed using both step normalized and wall clock normalized criteria to capture exploration effectiveness and practical computational cost.
