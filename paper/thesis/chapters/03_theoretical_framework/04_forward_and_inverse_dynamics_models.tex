\section{Forward and Inverse Dynamics Models}
\label{sec:forward_and_inverse_dynamics_models}

The latent dynamics module contains a forward predictor and an inverse predictor.
The forward predictor
\begin{equation}
    f_\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
\end{equation}
receives the current latent representation and action, then predicts the next latent representation.
The inverse predictor
\begin{equation}
    g_\xi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\Delta(\mathcal{A})
\end{equation}
receives consecutive latent representations and predicts the action that caused the transition.
For discrete action spaces, $\Delta(\mathcal{A})$ is a categorical distribution over actions.
For continuous action spaces, the inverse model is represented as a diagonal Gaussian likelihood over real-valued actions.

The per-transition forward loss is the mean-squared prediction error in feature space,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t)
    = \frac{1}{d}\left\lVert f_\psi(z_t,a_t)-z_{t+1}\right\rVert_2^2.
    \label{eq:forward_loss}
\end{equation}
This quantity is also used as the transition-level prediction error,
\begin{equation}
    e_t=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t).
    \label{eq:prediction_error}
\end{equation}
The scalar $e_t$ is not used directly as the final intrinsic reward, because high prediction error alone may correspond to stochastic or unlearnable dynamics.
Instead, it is used to estimate learning progress inside local regions of the latent space.

The inverse loss depends on the action space.
For a discrete action $a_t$, the inverse model produces logits over actions and is trained with cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)
    = -\log g_\xi(a_t\mid z_t,z_{t+1}).
\end{equation}
For a continuous action $a_t\in\mathbb{R}^{m}$, the inverse model produces a mean vector $\mu_\xi(z_t,z_{t+1})$ and diagonal log-standard-deviation vector $\ell_\xi(z_t,z_{t+1})$.
With $\sigma_\xi=\exp(\ell_\xi)$, the negative log-likelihood is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}(t)
    = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}
    \left[
        \frac{(a_{t,j}-\mu_{\xi,j})^2}{\sigma_{\xi,j}^2}
        +2\ell_{\xi,j}
        +\log(2\pi)
    \right].
\end{equation}

The combined dynamics loss is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{dyn}}(t)
    = \beta_{\mathrm{fwd}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fwd}}(t)
    + \beta_{\mathrm{inv}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{inv}}(t),
    \label{eq:dynamics_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{\mathrm{fwd}}>0$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{inv}}>0$ are weighting coefficients.
The forward term supplies a predictive error signal for learning-progress estimation, while the inverse term discourages representations that ignore action-relevant information.
This combination supports an intrinsic reward based on model improvement without making raw prediction error the sole driver of exploration.
