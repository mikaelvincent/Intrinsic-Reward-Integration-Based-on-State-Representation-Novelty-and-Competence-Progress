\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

Sparse and delayed rewards make reinforcement learning difficult because the agent may not receive enough external feedback to discover useful behavior during early training.
Intrinsic-reward methods address this problem by adding auxiliary exploration signals, but these signals can introduce their own failure modes.
In particular, novelty- and prediction-error-based rewards may remain large in regions where the dynamics are noisy, difficult to predict, or not useful for solving the task.
The technical problem addressed in this study is how to construct an intrinsic reward that promotes useful exploration while reducing the influence of unproductive curiosity.

The study focuses on the following research question:

\begin{quote}
How can feature-space impact and region-local learning progress be combined, and selectively gated, to improve reinforcement-learning exploration under sparse and continuous-control settings?
\end{quote}

This question is investigated through the design and evaluation of a PPO-compatible intrinsic-reward method that estimates impact and learning progress from on-policy experience.
The method is assessed against standard reinforcement-learning baselines and intrinsic-reward approaches using sample-efficiency, reliability, and wall-clock efficiency criteria.

\subsection{General Objective}
\label{subsec:general_objective}

The general objective of this study is to develop and evaluate a gated learning-progress intrinsic-reward method for reinforcement learning that encourages productive exploration by combining feature-space impact with localized model-improvement estimates.

\subsection{Specific Objectives}
\label{subsec:specific_objectives}

The specific objectives of the study are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formulate an intrinsic-reward function that combines representation-space transition impact with region-local learning progress.
    \item Develop an online latent-space partitioning procedure for maintaining local prediction-error statistics during policy training.
    \item Define a gating mechanism that suppresses intrinsic reward in regions characterized by persistently high prediction error and low learning progress.
    \item Integrate the GLPE intrinsic reward with PPO using augmented rewards and scheduled intrinsic-reward scaling.
    \item Compare GLPE against PPO, ICM, RND, RIDE, and RIAC on a benchmark suite containing sparse-reward and continuous-control environments.
    \item Evaluate performance using learning curves, final-checkpoint return, step-normalized area under the curve, thresholded reliability, and wall-clock efficiency.
    \item Analyze ablations that isolate the roles of impact, learning progress, and gating in the resulting exploration behavior.
\end{enumerate}
